Review of Continuous Probability Distributions

In this review, we will practice with two important continuous probability distributions: the uniform distribution and the normal distribution. For each example, focus on identifying the type of distribution, the given information, and whether the problem is asking for a probability or a cutoff value.

Uniform Distribution Review

A uniform distribution has equally likely values over an interval. For a continuous uniform distribution on the interval \([a,b]\) it is good to draw an image of a rectangular box and shade the area corresponding to the probability of interest. The height of the box is always \(\cfrac{1}{b-a}\).

Example : Uniform Distribution Application

A city bus arrives at a stop at a random time between 2:00 p.m. and 2:20 p.m. Assume the arrival time is uniformly distributed over this interval. What is the probability that the bus arrives between 2:05 p.m. and 2:12 p.m.?

Solution

Standard Normal Distribution Review

The standard normal distribution is a normal distribution with mean \(\mu=0\) and standard deviation \(\sigma=1\). Values from this distribution are typically called z-scores since \(z\)-scores are standardized values that indicate how many standard deviations a data point is from the mean.

Example : Probability of \(z\) or Greater

Find the probability of getting a z-score of \(1.43\) or greater. Round to three decimal places or one decimal place as a percentage.

Solution

Example : Probability Between Two Z-Scores

Find the probability that a standard normal random variable is between \(z=-0.84\) and \(z=1.67\). Round to three decimal places or one decimal place as a percentage.

Solution

Find the z-score such that the area to the left is \(0.91\). Round the z-score to two decimal places.

Applications of Non-Standard Normal Distributions

For non-standard normal distributions, the mean and standard deviation are not \(0\) and \(1\). These application problems may ask for probabilities or for values that cut off a given percentage.

Example : Army Height Requirements for Women

Suppose women's heights are normally distributed with a mean of \(64.5\) inches and a standard deviation of \(2.5\) inches.

  1. If the army requires women to be at least \(58\) inches tall and no more than \(80\) inches tall, what percentage of women meet this requirement?
  2. Suppose the army changes its requirement so that it denies the shortest \(2\%\) and the tallest \(1\%\) of women. What are the new minimum and maximum allowable heights?

Solution

Example : Exam Scores Application

Scores on a national certification exam are normally distributed with a mean of \(72\) and a standard deviation of \(8\).

  1. What is the probability that a randomly selected student scores between \(68\) and \(85\)?
  2. Find the exam score that cuts off the top \(12\%\) of all scores.
